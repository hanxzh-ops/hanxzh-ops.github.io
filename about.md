I'm Hanxiao Zhang — a mechanical engineer who likes to take an idea all the way from a sketch on a whiteboard to a part you can hold, run, and test. I'm happiest where the hard constraints live: tolerances that have to stack up, a casting that has to be machinable, a controller that has to run on real hardware, a deadline that has to be met.

My foundation is **product design and manufacturing**. I've run projects end-to-end — market survey, concept selection, CAD, prototyping, and a sold-out 20-unit production run for a rotary calendar; a food-safe rotary-vane pump taken from a quantified design brief through FEA/CFD, a full GD&T tolerance package, a cast-then-machine manufacturing plan, and a leak-tested validation build; and a transformable carry-on that mechanically expands between compact and high-capacity travel modes. I think in DFM, ASME Y14.5 GD&T, BOMs, and configuration-controlled documentation, because those are what turn a clever prototype into something a shop can actually build.

What I bring beyond the drawing board is a **mechatronics, controls, and simulation edge**. I've built a full locomotion-and-manipulation stack on a Unitree Go2 quadruped — RL sim-to-real transfer fused with MPC and onboard sensing for a cooperative human-robot carrying demo; a physics-simulated hexapod with analytical kinematics and a real-time tripod gait in MuJoCo; and a two-layer model-predictive guidance system that reroutes a damaged aircraft toward a survivable landing under degraded control authority. On the factory side I've written real automation tooling: an image-driven pipeline that converts 2D circuit schematics into Haas/Fanuc-ready G-code, and an Arduino-controlled rubber-batching system for Vibram that turns text recipes into precise dispensing commands. I also enjoy the data side of engineering — a recent study of mine compared 15 machine-learning models to predict Formula 1 race outcomes, where catching a subtle data-leakage bug mattered more than any single model.

That mix is the point. I can sit with a controls engineer and talk MPC and reinforcement learning, then walk to the shop floor and talk fits, finishes, and fixturing — and write the documentation that keeps both honest.

## What I work with

**Design & manufacturing:** SolidWorks, CAD/CAM, DFM, ASME Y14.5 GD&T, tolerance stack-up, FEA & CFD, CNC milling/turning, casting, sheet metal, prototyping, metrology, BOM & ECO documentation.

**Robotics & controls:** Model Predictive Control, reinforcement learning (PPO), inverse/forward kinematics, gait generation, sim-to-real, sensor fusion, MuJoCo, Isaac Lab, Pinocchio, CasADi, OSQP, ROS 2.

**Software & data:** Python, C++, OpenCV, NumPy/pandas, scikit-learn, Tkinter, convex optimization, machine learning.
